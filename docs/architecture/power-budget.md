# Power Architecture and Budgeting

## 1. Power Tree Overview (Discrete Daisy-Chain)

To give us absolute hardware-level control over power sequencing and consolidate the BOM to an extreme degree, this board completely abandons integrated PMICs in favor of a **fully discrete, daisy-chained power architecture**. 

The board accepts a wide-voltage DC input (9V - 24V nominal) and generates every system voltage using the **exact same switching regulator IC**.

### The "One Regulator" Strategy
We will use the **MPS MP2315S** (or equivalent, e.g., TI TPS54302) for every single power rail. 
*   **Specs:** 4.5V to 24V Input, 3A Output, Synchronous Buck, TSOT23-8 Package.
*   **Why it works:** It has a highly accurate **0.800V reference voltage (Vref)**, an Enable (EN) pin, and a Power Good (PG) pin. Because Vref is 0.800V, it can generate *any* voltage from 0.80V up to 5.0V simply by changing two feedback resistors.
*   **BOM Consolidation:** We will place **8 identical MP2315S chips** on the board. They will all use the exact same input capacitors, output capacitors, and inductors. Only the two 0402 feedback resistors will change between them.

### Regulator Allocation

All regulators are powered directly from the 9V-24V Main Input (or cascaded from the 5V rail for efficiency).

| Regulator | Voltage | Feedback Resistors (R1/R2) | Destination |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Buck 1** | 5.00V | R1=52.5kΩ, R2=10kΩ | USB VBUS, CAN-FD Transceivers |
| **Buck 2** | 3.30V | R1=31.25kΩ, R2=10kΩ | System 3.3V I/O (QSPI, USB PHY, LEDs) |
| **Buck 3** | 2.50V | R1=21.25kΩ, R2=10kΩ | 2x Ethernet PHY Analog (`VDDA2P5`) |
| **Buck 4** | 1.80V | R1=12.5kΩ, R2=10kΩ | System 1.8V I/O (`VDD`, WiFi, Eth MAC) |
| **Buck 5** | 1.10V | R1=3.75kΩ, R2=10kΩ | LPDDR4 Core (`VDD2`, `VDDQ`) |
| **Buck 6** | 1.00V | R1=2.5kΩ, R2=10kΩ | 2x Ethernet PHY Digital (`VDD1P0`) |
| **Buck 7** | 0.82V | R1=250Ω, R2=10kΩ | STM32MP257 `VDDCORE` & `VDDCPU` |
| **Buck 8** | 0.80V | R1=0Ω, R2=Open | STM32MP257 `VDDGPU` (GPU & NPU) |

*(Note: `VREFDDR` for the LPDDR4 is strictly 0.55V at ~10mA, which will be generated by a simple resistor divider from the 1.10V rail, buffered by a tiny LDO or op-amp).*

## 2. Power Good (PG) Daisy-Chain Sequencing

The STM32MP257 requires voltages to come up in a specific order. With discrete regulators, we achieve this flawlessly in hardware without any software intervention or NVM flashing. 

We daisy-chain the **Power Good (PG)** pin of one regulator directly to the **Enable (EN)** pin of the next regulator in the sequence. 

**Boot Sequence:**
1.  **Main Power Applied (9V-24V):** The 5.0V, 3.3V, and 2.5V regulators are enabled simultaneously via an RC delay or supervisor.
2.  **Step 1:** The 3.3V `PG` pin connects to the 1.8V `EN` pin.
3.  **Step 2:** The 1.8V `PG` pin connects to the 1.1V `EN` pin.
4.  **Step 3:** The 1.1V `PG` pin connects to the 0.82V and 0.80V `EN` pins.
5.  **Step 4:** The 0.82V `PG` pin connects to the `POR_B` (Power-On Reset) of the STM32MP257, booting the processor only when all rails are 100% stable.

*This gives you total, granular control. If you want to delay a rail, you just add a small capacitor to its EN pin.*

## 3. Power Consumption Calculations

We size the regulators to ensure they can handle the absolute maximum worst-case power draw. The MP2315S is rated for **3 Amps**.

### Maximum Load per Rail
1.  **5.0V Rail:** ~2.1A (10.5W)
    *   3x CAN-FD (`VCC`): ~135mA
    *   External USB 2.0 + 3.0: ~2000mA 
2.  **3.3V Rail:** ~0.4A (1.3W)
    *   WiFi/BT `VBAT`: ~300mA TX Peak
    *   STM32MP257 USB PHY: ~50mA
    *   QSPI Flash: ~25mA
3.  **2.5V Rail:** ~0.2A (0.5W)
    *   2x Eth PHY Analog: ~172mA
4.  **1.8V Rail:** ~0.5A (0.9W)
    *   STM32MP257 `VDD` & Analog: ~400mA
    *   LPDDR4 `VDD1`: ~80mA
5.  **1.1V Rail:** ~2.0A (2.2W)
    *   LPDDR4 `VDD2` & `VDDQ`: ~2.0A peak
6.  **1.0V Rail:** ~0.3A (0.3W)
    *   2x Eth PHY Digital: ~216mA
7.  **0.82V Rail:** ~2.3A (1.9W)
    *   STM32MP257 `VDDCORE` & `VDDCPU`: ~2300mA peak
8.  **0.80V Rail:** ~1.5A (1.2W)
    *   STM32MP257 `VDDGPU`: ~1500mA peak

**Calculations Conclusion:**
Every single rail draws less than 2.3 Amps absolute peak. Since our universal regulator (MP2315S) is rated for **3.0 Amps continuous**, we have sufficient headroom on every rail without needing to upgrade to larger ICs. 

Total system power is ~18.8 Watts, which pulls roughly ~1.5 Amps from a 12V battery.

## 4. Extreme BOM Consolidation & Thermals

To minimize assembly costs on LCSC/JLCPCB, we will enforce strict limits on the variety of passive components.

### Inductors
*   **Selected Part:** 2.2 µH or 3.3 µH, 3A+, 2016 metric (0806 imperial) footprint (e.g., Sunlord MWSA201612).
*   **Why:** We buy exactly **8 of the identical inductors** for the board. 

### Capacitors
We will restrict the decoupling and bulk capacitors to a maximum of 4 distinct part numbers:
1.  **0.1 µF (100nF) 0402 10V:** Local high-frequency decoupling for every IC pin.
2.  **1.0 µF 0402 10V:** Medium-frequency decoupling.
3.  **10 µF 0603 35V:** Universal input capacitor for all 8 regulators (can withstand up to 24V inputs).
4.  **22 µF 0603 10V:** Universal output bulk capacitor for all 8 regulators.

### Thermals
*   **Distributed Heat:** By abandoning a single PMIC, we prevent a significant localized "hotspot". The 8 regulators will be physically distributed around the perimeter of the STM32MP257, spreading the thermal load evenly across the PCB's copper planes.
*   **Vias:** Each regulator's exposed GND pad will have a 2x2 grid of thermal vias tying it to the internal ground layers.
